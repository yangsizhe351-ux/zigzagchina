FAQ｜Chengdu

How many days should I spend in Chengdu?

We recommend at least three full days to experience Chengdu at a comfortable pace. This gives you enough time for the pandas, Sichuan cuisine, traditional teahouses and some of the city's historic and contemporary neighborhoods without rushing from one attraction to the next. If you'd like to explore beyond the city, we suggest 5–10 days, combining Chengdu with destinations such as Dujiangyan, Mount Qingcheng, Leshan, Mount Emei or Western Sichuan.

When is the best time to visit the pandas?

Early morning is generally the best time. Giant pandas tend to be more active earlier in the day, making it a particularly rewarding time to visit. For our private journeys, we can arrange an early-morning panda experience and build the rest of your day around it, rather than trying to squeeze the Panda Base between other sightseeing stops.

Is Sichuan food very spicy?

It can be — but Sichuan cuisine is about much more than heat. One of its defining characteristics is málà: má refers to the distinctive tingling sensation of Sichuan pepper, while là refers to the heat of chilli. Beyond málà, Sichuan cuisine is known for its layers of aroma, texture and flavor. If you're not a fan of spicy food, we can tailor dining to your preferences, from mildly seasoned local dishes to a full Sichuan experience.

Do I need a private guide in Chengdu?

Not necessarily. But if you want to experience Chengdu beyond the main sights, a private journey can make a real difference. Chengdu is relatively easy to explore independently, but language, restaurant choices, local neighborhoods and small cultural details can sometimes be difficult to navigate without local knowledge. A private guide helps you find the restaurants locals go to, understand what you see, navigate unfamiliar neighborhoods and discover experiences you might otherwise miss. Importantly, your itinerary can adapt as your day changes.

Can I combine Chengdu with Western Sichuan?

Absolutely. For many travelers, this is where the journey becomes truly unforgettable. Chengdu offers food, culture and city life. Western Sichuan offers a different scale: snow-capped mountains, highland valleys, Tibetan cultural traditions, winding mountain roads and open landscapes. We can design journeys from a few days around Chengdu to a 7–12 day private journey through Chengdu and Western Sichuan.

FAQ｜Chongqing

How many days should I spend in Chongqing?

We recommend 2–3 full days for a first visit. This gives you enough time for the city’s iconic night views, mountain-city geography, local neighborhoods and a proper Chongqing hotpot. If you'd like to venture beyond the city, allow 4–5 days to add destinations such as Wulong Karst or the Dazu Rock Carvings.

Why does everyone call Chongqing an “8D city”?

Because in Chongqing, “up” and “down” can be as important as “left” and “right.” A road can pass above another road. A building entrance can be several floors above or below street level. And the city's famous monorail can appear to pass through a residential building at Liziba. This vertical geography is not a theme park effect; it's simply how Chongqing is built.

Is Chongqing really as spectacular at night as it looks online?

Yes — but the experience is better when you know where to look. Chongqing’s nightscape is one of its defining experiences: illuminated buildings climb the hillsides, bridges span the Yangtze and Jialing rivers, and Hongyadong glows above the waterfront. We can arrange riverside walks, private dining with a view, photography stops or quieter viewpoints to see different sides of the city.

Is Chongqing hotpot very spicy?

It can be — but you don't have to start at full intensity. Chongqing hotpot is famous for bold málà flavors, often built around chilli and beef tallow. For first-time visitors, we can help you choose the right spice level, introduce the dipping sauce and explain what you’re ordering when menus are in Chinese.

Do I need a private guide in Chongqing?

You don't have to — but local knowledge can make a big difference. Chongqing's unusual terrain can make simple journeys complicated. A short walk may involve steep stairs, multiple levels or a different entrance. A private guide helps you spend less time figuring out how to get there and more time experiencing why it is worth going there.

Is Chongqing only about Hongyadong and the monorail?

Definitely not. They are iconic for a reason, but they are only the introduction. Depending on your interests, you can explore mountain-city walking routes, historic neighborhoods, riverside districts, local markets, teahouses, food streets, Nanshan viewpoints, the Yangtze River and contemporary nightlife.

FAQ｜Chengdu + Chongqing

Can I visit Chengdu and Chongqing on the same trip?

Absolutely. In fact, the two cities make a strong combination. They are close enough to connect easily by high-speed rail, yet different enough to feel like two distinct experiences. Chengdu is relaxed, culinary and cultural; Chongqing is dramatic, vertical and cinematic.

How many days do I need for both Chengdu and Chongqing?

For a comfortable first trip, we recommend 6–8 days. A good balance is 3 days Chengdu + 3 days Chongqing, with extra time for places like Sanxingdui, Dujiangyan, Leshan, Wulong or Western Sichuan. For 4–5 days, it is still possible, but the itinerary will be faster-paced.

Which city should I visit first — Chengdu or Chongqing?

There is no wrong answer, but the order changes the trip's rhythm. Starting in Chengdu creates a gentler beginning through pandas, food and tea. Finishing in Chongqing gives a dramatic finale with riverside skylines, steep streets and city energy after dark.

Will visiting both cities feel repetitive?

Not at all. Chengdu and Chongqing are both vibrant, but their energy is different. Chengdu can feel slower and more relaxed. Chongqing feels more intense and vertical. The contrast is part of the experience.

Should I combine Chengdu and Chongqing with Western Sichuan too?

You can, but choose your priorities carefully. Chengdu + Chongqing already gives a rich combination of food, culture, city life and dramatic landscapes. Adding Western Sichuan changes the scale with mountains, highlands and long scenic drives. For 7–8 days, we usually keep the focus on Chengdu and Chongqing; for 10–14 days or more, Western Sichuan becomes a smoother addition.

Is Chengdu + Chongqing suitable for families and older travelers?

Yes, with thoughtful pacing. Both cities suit families and multi-generational groups, but Chongqing's hills and stairs can be physically demanding. For these travelers, we recommend private transport, carefully chosen hotels and flexible schedules.

What should I know before combining Chengdu and Chongqing?

The biggest mistake is trying to do too much. The cities are close geographically but deserve different rhythms. Don't treat them as two boxes to tick. Give Chengdu time for food, tea and neighborhoods. Give Chongqing time for its nightscape and mountain-city energy. Leave room for unexpected discoveries.
